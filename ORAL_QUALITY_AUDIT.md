## Batch 1 - Schizophrenia & Psychoses

Reviewed and revised 30/129 oral answers:

- `1Aa1` symptom dimensions: aligned with Oxford positive, negative, disorganization and cognitive groupings; added affective/depressive differential emphasis.
- `1Aa2` Schneider first-rank symptoms: added exact symptom list, DSM-5 status, and Oxford's 10-20% occurrence in other psychotic disorders.
- `1Aa3` DSM-5 vs ICD-10: clarified duration, functional impairment, first-rank weighting, subtypes and schizotypal placement.
- `1Aa4` prodromal psychosis assessment: added collateral history, differential diagnosis, risk assessment and Oxford conversion caution of under 25% over 2-3 years.
- `1Ab1` first psychotic episode management: added risk triage, organic/substance exclusion, low-dose antipsychotic start, no loading doses and psychosocial work.
- `1Ab2` response/duration: replaced broad estimate with Oxford-supported two-thirds response, 2-week nonresponse signal, relapse figures and maintenance duration guidance.
- `1Ab3` DUP: expanded prognostic significance and Oxford's four possible explanations.
- `1Ab4` prognosis: added poor prognostic factors and long-term recovery figures.
- `1Ab5` gender differences: added 1.4:1 male:female ratio, age peaks and caution about estrogen explanation.
- `1Ab6` suicide: added 5% lifetime suicide risk, mortality gap and schizophrenia-specific risk framing.
- `1Ag1` to `1Ag4` cognition: added domains, one-standard-deviation average impairment, first-episode/premorbid timing, MATRICS and treatment limits.
- `1Ad1` to `1Ad8` treatment resistance/clozapine: tightened TRS definition, adequate trial criteria, clozapine indications, monitoring, adverse effects, levels, discontinuation logic, hypersalivation treatment and glutamate/NMDA model.
- `1Ae1` to `1Ae5` mechanisms/imaging: revised dopamine/glutamate explanation, pathways, negative symptoms, psychosocial risk numbers and imaging findings.
- `1Az1` to `1Az3` psychosocial treatment/depot/adherence: added family work, expressed emotion, depot relapse-prevention data and adherence rates.

## Batch 2 - Delirium

Reviewed and revised 8/129 oral answers:

- `1B1` definition/features: added clouding of consciousness, hyperactive/hypoactive forms, 24-hour fluctuation and CAM screening.
- `1B2` causes: expanded predisposing and precipitating factors; added medication, infection, electrolytes, hypoxia, pain, constipation, restraints and a usable `PINCH ME` mnemonic.
- `1B3` differential diagnosis: clarified attention/consciousness versus memory versus mood pattern.
- `1B4` psychiatric patient risk: emphasized polypharmacy, sedatives/anticholinergics, substances, withdrawal and diagnostic overshadowing.
- `1B5` restraint: reframed restraint as a precipitating factor and last-resort safety intervention.
- `1B6` delirium tremens: added timing around 48 hours, 3-4 day course, autonomic/electrolyte features, thiamine and benzodiazepine-first treatment.
- `1B7` violent patient in ED: added safety, de-escalation, organic causes, rapid tranquillization, monitoring and post-incident review.
- `1B8` hospital delirium drug: clarified that causal/non-pharmacological treatment is primary; haloperidol only for severe agitation/distressing psychotic symptoms, typically 1-5 mg/day or less in older adults, with major cautions.

Next suggested batch: `1Ca1`-`1Cd4` Mood disorders and suicide.
